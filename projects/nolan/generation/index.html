The standing text-only lock is the original Star Wars prompt: Commander Pillar walking a dark torrential battlefield at night. The scene is a wide hunt, a mask close-up, a rifle stalk, a storm POV, a lightning reveal, a handheld pistol draw, a red bolt past the camera, a green saber deflection, a dodge, a rifle-saber ignition with the red crossguard blade, and a mid-air clash that ends just before they collide. No music; only rain, storm, footsteps, blasters, and saber ignitions.

Locked prompt

Text-only runs of the current lock use this wording unchanged. If a run uses references or a different prompt, record that protocol on the run instead of rewriting this lock.

A sci-fi soldier from Star Wars is walking through a dark torrential rainstorm at night.

He is a commander in the Galactic Empire named Pillar, wearing all-black armor with a tattered black cloak around his shoulders. He is wearing a helmet/mask that is also all black that looks similar to Kylo Ren's helmet, with a few differences: there is no chrome, it's all black. It's also more detailed with more small design aspects in the face of the mask, and the respirator thingy is slightly narrower than on Kylo's mask. Essentially, the mask looks scarier and more intense than Kylo's.

The armor itself is matte black, battle-worn, and sparse, with worn-out black fabric elements here and there.

He is lean, maybe 150lbs but pure muscle, which is not visible under the armor but his body shape indicated strength for his size. 6ft tall.

His posture as he walks is that of a stalking animal - slight forward hunch, head poking forward as he hunts for his prey. Slowly, confidently, dangerously.... deadly.

On his left hip is a black lightsaber hilt of minimal design. On his thighs are sheathed battle knives. He is holding is rifle, which is about 3ft long of simple design with a large barrel - maybe 2 inches in diameter. it does NOT have a shoulder mount for the butt - the butt of the rifle looks similar to the very tip of the barrel and protrudes only a few inches past the handle of the gun.

He is holding it across his torso casually, but tense - ready to fire at any moment, but not eager to.

Around him in the distance, some more visible than others, are stormtroopers in their traditional white armor, also searching for targets but with their blasters raised and ready to fire.

The darkness and the storm around them make the image difficult to see - the winds are extremely high and the rain thick, with dust from the fresh battle still billowing in the air before becoming the mud beneath their feat. On the ground around them are lifeless bodies - most of which are rebel soldiers wearing whatever battle-ready outfits they could piece together, like guerrilla terrorists only with decent helmets of varying styles. Many of them are aliens. Among their bodies are also those of dead stormtroopers, with a ratio of about 5 rebels to 1 stormtrooper.

This was the setup for the scene. Here's how it plays out:

The imperial soldiers all make their way through this stormy battlefield, looking for surviving stormtroopers and executing surviving rebels (you can occasionally see a stormtrooper in the distance stop to fire a bright red blaster bolt into the ground where the rebel survivor is). the scene starts wide, the suddenly cuts in to a close up of Pillar's face (mask) as he slowly looks directly ahead (from looking at the ground) past and slightly to the left of the camera.

Then we cut to a wider side-on view of Pillar as he raises is blaster rifle and starts slowly and stealthfully stepping forward again, ready to fire at a moment's notice.

The we cut to his POV looking off in the distance of the storm, unable to see anything in the dark tempest other than the faint outline of mountains in the distance.

then suddenly lightening flashes and we can briefly make out the shape of a man not far in the distance standing squarely facing us before the sky goes dark again.

We cut to pillar again, handheld camera movement tracking shot, as he lowers his blaster rifle and holds in his left hand, with his hand wrapped around the intersection of the handle and the butt end of the barrel so the the long end of the rifle is facing backwards in his grip (because his blaster double's as a lightsaber, which we will see later). He then reaches behind him (camera closely follows his hand throughout this entire shot) and pulls one of the two blaster pistols holstered on the back of his belt. He then raises it in front of him straight out and pauses before firing.

We cut back to the POV view of the storm again for a few seconds as suspense builds.

Then back to Pillar, face-on, as the camera pushes in slowly. then suddenly he fires the blaster pistol, the laser bolt shooting right past the camera in a flurry of red energy.

Instantly cut back to the wide side-view shot so we see the bolt zip from the blaster pistol and off-frame to the left.

Then cut to the storm POV again as the bolt soars into the abyss. after 1.5 second the bolt reaches the mysterious figure, who suddenly ignights his green lightsaber, illuminating the darkness and the storm around him in the distance, to deflect the red blaster bolt directly back at Pillar.

We cut back to the side view, tighter shot this time, as Pillar moves his head just in time to dodge the deflected bolt and then lunges forward and out of frame (to the left).

Wide shot shows him igniting his own blaster rifle/lightsaber, with a crackling red lightsaber blade erupting from the butt of the rifle AND a shorter blade, like a crossguard, erupting from the base of the handle/grip of the rifle so it forms a 45 degree angle with the main blade.

All the while, Pillar continues to fire a volley of bolts from his pistol towards the enemy before spinning his lightsaber once and launching himself into the air, hurtling towards the enemy.

The one last shot: a VERY wide shot side-on of Pillar mid-air lunging towards the man with the green lightsaber (the man left side of frame, Pillar right side of frame) with both their blades illuminating the stormy wrath around them. The Jedi (the man with the green lightsaber) suddenly leaps to attack Pillar mid-air, and the scene ends just before they collide.

The sound design of this scene has no music, only the realistic sounds of the rain and storms, footsteps, blaster shots, and lightsaber ignitions.

What changed

This is the first full Blender version of the locked action in this workstream: a 41-second, twelve-shot scene, not a short image-generated substitute. The approved helmet and accepted rifle mesh and shader data stay. The visor stays pure unlit black. The rifle stays in the left hand, barrel backward for the saber. Only the rear main blade and one angled grip crossguard ignite, both red. The right hand draws and fires a pistol from the back of the belt.

The lean clothed body was rebuilt from two new primary references, not by reusing a rejected earlier character unchanged. The armored reference governs the all-black outfit. The bare-chested reference informs underlying proportions. The scene adds wet terrain, mountain ridges, geometry rain, animated ground dust, six searching white-armored patrol figures, twelve fallen combatants logged as 10 rebels to 2 white-armored, a distant Jedi, bolts, and motivated saber light.

The build stages the principal locked beats: an uninterrupted right-hand draw, suspense point-of-view shots, an immediate side cut after the first shot, a green deflection timed 1.5 seconds into the flight point of view, a dodge, red two-blade ignition, a volley, one weapon spin, a launch, and a very wide airborne ending before contact.

Methods

This is procedural Blender work, not neural training or fine-tuning, and not a new generative-video model.

  • Blender Python meshes.
  • Analytic two-bone human IK, baked at 24 fps, on a deformable human skeleton. Separate prop-grasp transforms. Weighted armature deformation.
  • Cape wind shape keys, not a cloth simulation.
  • Twelve named moving cameras and hard-cut markers.
  • CPU Cycles at native 960×540, 12 samples, with adaptive sampling, denoising, and motion blur.
  • Procedural stereo storm, foley, and fictional blaster and saber sound. No score, no voices, and no purchased assets.

Comparison with generative r2

  • This cut holds fixed geometry and explicit staging and timing. R2 is a reference-assisted generation of separate beats.
  • Realistic anatomy, acting, and wet-surface detail are weaker here.
  • There is no helmet runoff simulation. Rain is tiled streak geometry. A relayed note on r2’s rain close-up, especially dripping edges, is a direction for wet-surface detail. This Blender sequence was not rerendered to imitate that result.
  • None of that is a measured improvement, or decline, of the generative model.

Geometry checks

These are build-log checks, not a judgment of how the picture reads. The locked prompt was embedded unchanged. Canonical helmet and rifle sources were unchanged. The log records twelve cameras, 984 frames, a black emission-only visor, the rifle in the left hand, the pistol in the right hand, no front blade, and two red blades. It also records the green blade off in the first lightning reveal, a 1.5-second deflection timing, a returned bolt missing the helmet, and both figures airborne at a pre-contact end. The accepted rifle measures about 1.047 m, not the prompt’s 3 ft. Passing these checks does not establish film-quality acting or visual likeness.

Review

Editron’s later review of the finished movie:

  • The mechanics read stiff and floaty.
  • The pistol draw is obscured, and the cape clips.
  • The enemy reveal is weak.
  • On screen, the back-facing rifle was read as slung, and extra blades appeared to be there. The geometry checks say the rifle stays anchored in the left hand and that there are exactly two blade meshes. That is a screen-readability problem, not a proven extra mesh.
  • The black visor stayed consistent.
  • Atmosphere, framing, and the final pre-impact airborne composition communicate the intent of the shot.

Audio is in the file. The reviewer could not assess it. This page does not call the sound listening-reviewed.

Lessons

  • A fixed mesh identity is not film-quality acting.
  • Numeric geometry checks do not make a shot visually legible. Grip, barrel direction, and blade count can be correct in the file and still be misread on screen.
  • Anchor the reversed rifle at one explicit grip. Check the barrel against the actor’s forward direction in the rig, instead of guessing screen direction after a cut.
  • Grow saber meshes from emitter-aligned pivots. Scaling world Z alone on an angled blade leaves a compressed glow and can show a green blade too early in the lightning.
  • Use a real bolt-and-blade intersection. A flash near the opponent is not a deflection by itself.
  • Put Pillar airborne on the final cut and stop before collision.
  • A first-person camera inside a genuinely black helmet sees black. Place the point of view just forward of the visor, and do not brighten the visor shader to compensate.
  • On CPU renders, resume frames that are already finished. Do not launch a second multi-hour render of the same frames after an interruption.
  • The Blender workflow was longer than the generated run. The log does not support a precise time or cost comparison.

Limitations

Background soldiers, the Jedi’s face and robes, fallen-figure variety, armor tailoring, and acting stay simplified. Cape motion is shape keys, not cloth simulation. Rain is not fluid or surface runoff. The downward ground shot is staged simply, without a detailed surviving-rebel reaction. Leaps are keyed paths, not a gravity solve. Native 540p and a 12-sample render can show noise and denoising. Sound is synthesized foley, not field recordings. Stills in the build notes already showed toy-like joints and cloth, and some arm and cape occlusion in the hand-follow shot. The later review is what confirms stiffness, float, clipping, and the weak reveal in motion.

Camera: 35mm full-frame equivalent, eye level, 1.6 meters above the pier boards, always on the pier itself. No drone, no view from the water or the sky, no fisheye, no telephoto compression, no handheld shake, no zoom.
