CyberBoard

A DIY ultra-minimal one-wheel electric board built from a used hoverboard.

Lead designer & builder · Oct 2026 – present

  • Electromechanical design
  • FreeCAD
  • 3D printing
  • ESP32
  • Sensors
  • Aluminum fabrication
  • Power (36V→5V)

Problem

Build a rideable single-wheel board with a smooth, sharp-edged brushed-aluminum look on a tight budget and basic tools.

Constraints

  • Budget $150–200 total
  • Tools: older 3D printer (PLA), FreeCAD, household drill
  • Serviceable: top plates on screws into rivet nuts
  • Treaded tire for wet roads and snow

Key result

Not built yet. Donor board bought ($40); its battery read ~0.7 V on the bench, so a 36V 4.4Ah pack was ordered. $88.99 of the $150–200 budget spent.

STAR

Situation

Commercial one-wheel boards cost far more than my budget.

Task

Design and build one from a ~$40 used 6.5" 36V hoverboard: parts, design decisions and fabrication.

Action

  • Wheel: 11x7.10-5 treaded go-kart tire with the two hub motors machined to act as the rim
  • Frame: aluminum rails with a ~27.5 mm riser (Rev A.3) so a larger battery fits under the deck
  • Power: replaced the dead 2.0Ah donor pack with a 36V 4.4Ah 10S2P pack (XT60, BMS)
  • Lighting: ESP32 reads a hall sensor (MPU6050 backup) so the front end face glows white and the rear red; 36V→5V buck
  • Bent sheet-aluminum splash guards; custom rail sticker

Result

First bench check: donor pack measured ~0.7 V (below BMS cutoff, so treated as dead). No ride data yet. Success criteria: rides stably, LEDs switch correctly with direction, stays within budget, top plates removable for service.

Design decisions

DecisionOptions / choiceTradeoff
Hoverboard donorReuse motors, controller and charger vs buying partsFar cheaper; tied to stock electronics
Bigger battery + riserKeep the 2.0Ah donor pack vs a 36V 4.4Ah packDonor pack dead and too small for range; riser raises the deck so the new pack fits
Treaded tireTreaded vs slick go-kart tireBetter wet/snow grip; ice still needs studs
Screws into rivet nutsServiceable top plates vs rivetedMore parts, but easy access to electronics
