CyberBoard

Problem

Build a rideable single-wheel board with a smooth, sharp-edged brushed-aluminum look on a tight budget and basic tools.

Constraints

  • Budget $150–200 total
  • Tools: older 3D printer (PLA), FreeCAD, household drill
  • Serviceable: top plates on screws into rivet nuts
  • Treaded go-kart tire, 260 mm OD × 170 mm wide

Key result

Teardown is underway. The first stator came out after about an hour stuck in the rotor. The axle is measured, Rev B is drawn, and axle mount D-011 is proposed, not chosen. The tire is picked and not here yet. No finished-board ride. $88.99 of $150–200 spent.

Failure and fix

The tire would not come off the hub, so I cut it off and separated the boards. With the motor open, the stator stayed in the rotor. The magnets hold it hard. After about an hour I pulled the first stator out by hand. It has to come out: the two motors join closed face to closed face, with a printed spacer and M6 rods, and the axles point out to the rails. Motor 2 is next.

Proposed axle mount

Proposed, not chosen. D-011 is on the Rev B sheets so the measured axle has a place to go. I have not selected it.

Per side, D-011 is two 1/4 in aluminum key plates on the faces of the 20×40 rail, each with an open-bottom D-slot, plus a keeper block under the axle and a shaft collar for axial location. The axle tip stays open so the motor cable can unplug there. The wheel drops out after the keeper bolts come off. Putting the rails above the axle is what raises the deck from ~187 mm to ~217 mm (EST.).

STAR

Situation

Commercial one-wheel boards cost far more than my budget.

Task

Design and build one from a ~$40 used 6.5" 36V hoverboard.

Action

  • Two hub motors as the rim, closed face to closed face, for a 260 × 170 mm treaded go-kart tire
  • Rev B rails sit above the measured D-profile axle. Mount D-011 is only a proposal
  • Dead donor pack replaced by a measured 36V 4.4Ah pack under the deck
  • Direction-aware end lights, and top plates that come off for the electronics

Result

The donor board runs on the new pack. Teardown has started, the axle is measured, and Rev B is drawn. No finished-board ride data yet.
