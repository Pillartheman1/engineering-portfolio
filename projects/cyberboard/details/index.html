Status

In progress. Teardown started 16 Nov 2026: the tire was cut off, the boards are separated, and the first stator came out of the rotor after about an hour. The axle is measured. Rev B (17 Nov) uses that axle and the measured pack. Axle mount D-011 is drawn and proposed, not chosen. A treaded go-kart tire, 260 mm OD × 170 mm wide, is picked and not here yet. The air valve is planned through the printed spacer band. The donor still runs on the new 36V 4.4Ah pack ($48.99): both motors drove and it self-balanced on 15 Nov. The old pack was dead (~0.7 V). Spent so far: $88.99 of $150–200. Motor width, spacer thickness, and the tire on the motors are still estimates (EST.).

Problem & goal

Build a rideable single-wheel (Onewheel-style) board from a used hoverboard, with an ultra-minimal, sharp-edged smooth-aluminum look and direction-aware LED lighting, for $150–200 total using basic tools.

System architecture

Mechanical

  • Two aluminum 20×40 mm rails form the frame. On Rev B they sit above the axle. The mount that puts them there is D-011 (key plates, a keeper, and a shaft collar), and it is proposed, not chosen
  • Wheel: two hub motors joined closed face to closed face with a printed spacer and M6 rods through the spoke pads. The air valve runs through the printed spacer band on a short metal tubeless stem that is still to buy. The spacer is porous, so the band and both motor seams have to be sealed
  • Decks: 1/8" aluminum diamond plate, screwed into rivet nuts so they lift off for service
  • Splash guards: thin (~1/16") bent sheet-aluminum guards, 2–3" tall, at each wheel cutout edge (full fender dropped)
  • Riser: 25–30 mm spacer on top of the rails only, kept on Rev B, to clear the larger battery
  • Printed parts split to fit the printer bed; split parts bonded with two-part plastic epoxy, no CA glue on load-bearing joints
  • Printed parts: spacers, bumpers, gyro activator cap, gyro mounting template; PLA ~549 g, ~16 h

Electrical & power

  • Stock mainboard and one gyro board; the gyro input is rewired so a single gyro drives both motors (no custom firmware)
  • 36V → 5V buck converter feeds the ESP32 and LED strips
  • New 36V 4.4Ah pack between the rails under the deck, made to fit by the ~27.5 mm riser; mainboard, gyro and ESP32 under the front deck, in sealed enclosures
  • Cold weather: expect reduced output and range; do not charge below 0 °C

Control & sensors

  • Primary direction sensing: tap one hub motor's hall sensor lines; pulse order gives forward vs reverse
  • Backup: MPU6050 IMU (noisier, because the board is always tilting)
  • Hysteresis/debounce so colors swap only after a sustained reversal
  • Rider detect: floating foot plate on 3–4 springs or rubber bumpers presses the stock foot button; stock wiring and logic kept
