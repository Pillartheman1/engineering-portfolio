Problem & goal

Build a rideable single-wheel (Onewheel-style) board from a used hoverboard, with an ultra-minimal, sharp-edged smooth-aluminum look and direction-aware LED lighting, for $150–200 total using basic tools.

System architecture

Mechanical

  • Two aluminum 20×40 mm rails form the frame; the hoverboard's axle-mount adapter is cut in half and reused, with M8 bolts
  • Wheel: two hub motors bolted side by side (M6 rods + 3D-printed spacer) with the rim lip machined off; sides sealed with automotive sealant and a 5 mm aluminum ring; Presta valve tapped into a motor cover
  • Decks: 1/8" aluminum diamond plate, screwed into rivet nuts so they lift off for service
  • Splash guards: thin (~1/16") bent sheet-aluminum guards, 2–3" tall, at each wheel cutout edge (full fender dropped)
  • Riser: 25–30 mm spacer on top of the rails only (Rev A.3) to clear the larger battery
  • Printed parts split to fit the printer bed; split parts bonded with two-part plastic epoxy, no CA glue on load-bearing joints
  • Printed parts (MakerWorld kit: spacers, bumpers, gyro activator cap, gyro mounting template; PLA ~549 g, ~16 h)

Electrical & power

  • Stock mainboard and one gyro board; the gyro input is rewired so a single gyro drives both motors (no custom firmware)
  • 36V → 5V buck converter feeds the ESP32 and LED strips
  • New 36V 4.4Ah pack between the rails under the deck, made to fit by the Rev A.3 riser; mainboard, gyro and ESP32 under the front deck, in sealed enclosures
  • Cold weather: expect reduced output and range; do not charge below 0 °C

Control & sensors

  • Primary direction sensing: tap one hub motor's hall sensor lines; pulse order gives forward vs reverse
  • Backup: MPU6050 IMU (noisier, because the board is always tilting)
  • Hysteresis/debounce so colors swap only after a sustained reversal
  • Rider detect: floating foot plate on 3–4 springs or rubber bumpers presses the stock foot button; stock wiring and logic kept

Design decisions

DecisionAlternatives consideredChoice & rationale
Donor drivetrainBuy motor/controller/battery separatelyReuse a used hoverboard: far cheaper; tied to stock electronics
BatteryKeep the 2.0Ah donor pack; ≤$25 used packNew 36V 4.4Ah 10S2P pack ($48.99): donor pack read ~0.7 V (dead) and was too small for range
Battery fitEnclosure hanging below the rails25–30 mm riser on the rails (Rev A.3): keeps ground clearance, pack sits under the deck
LookAngular faceted body with rails and visible fasteners (earlier concepts looked like a hovercraft)Concept v5/v6: all-smooth aluminum, sharp edges, lights only on the end faces, no visible screws
Donor sourceNew 6.5" boards $99–130; eBay used (mostly parts)Used local board $15–45: keeps budget; must be tested
WheelTire + rim kitTire only; the two motors are the rim, so a rim is wasted money
TreadSlick ($29.79–39.99)Treaded: wet and snow grip; costs more; ice still needs studs
Firmwarehoverboard-firmware-hack-FOCStock electronics, one gyro rewired: proven in the reference build, less risk
Foot triggerFSR / force sensorSpring-mounted plate pressing the stock button: no new electronics in the safety-critical circuit
Direction sensingMPU6050 IMUHall sensor tap primary (cleaner signal); IMU kept as backup
Deck material9 mm plywood (needs sealing), PVC (brittle in cold), HDPE/polycarbonate (flexy), G10 (expensive)1/8" aluminum diamond plate: stiff, weatherproof, available locally
Avionics accessDedicated hatch with captive/quarter-turn fastenersRemovable top plates with screws into rivet nuts: simpler, threads survive repeated removal
FenderFull fender; short 1–2" wedge guardsThin 2–3" bent sheet-aluminum guards: keep the look, still catch debris
Adhesive for split PLA partsCA glue; flexible plastic bonderRigid two-part plastic epoxy: fills seams, longer working time
Cosmetic marble slabSlabScrapped: heavy, brittle, cosmetic only
