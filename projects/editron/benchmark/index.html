Locked prompt

Text-only runs of the current lock use this wording unchanged. If a run uses references or a different prompt, record that protocol on the run instead of rewriting this lock.

A sci-fi soldier from Star Wars is walking through a dark torrential rainstorm at night.

He is a commander in the Galactic Empire named Pillar, wearing all-black armor with a tattered black cloak around his shoulders. He is wearing a helmet/mask that is also all black that looks similar to Kylo Ren's helmet, with a few differences: there is no chrome, it's all black. It's also more detailed with more small design aspects in the face of the mask, and the respirator thingy is slightly narrower than on Kylo's mask. Essentially, the mask looks scarier and more intense than Kylo's.

The armor itself is matte black, battle-worn, and sparse, with worn-out black fabric elements here and there.

He is lean, maybe 150lbs but pure muscle, which is not visible under the armor but his body shape indicated strength for his size. 6ft tall.

His posture as he walks is that of a stalking animal - slight forward hunch, head poking forward as he hunts for his prey. Slowly, confidently, dangerously.... deadly.

On his left hip is a black lightsaber hilt of minimal design. On his thighs are sheathed battle knives. He is holding is rifle, which is about 3ft long of simple design with a large barrel - maybe 2 inches in diameter. it does NOT have a shoulder mount for the butt - the butt of the rifle looks similar to the very tip of the barrel and protrudes only a few inches past the handle of the gun.

He is holding it across his torso casually, but tense - ready to fire at any moment, but not eager to.

Around him in the distance, some more visible than others, are stormtroopers in their traditional white armor, also searching for targets but with their blasters raised and ready to fire.

The darkness and the storm around them make the image difficult to see - the winds are extremely high and the rain thick, with dust from the fresh battle still billowing in the air before becoming the mud beneath their feat. On the ground around them are lifeless bodies - most of which are rebel soldiers wearing whatever battle-ready outfits they could piece together, like guerrilla terrorists only with decent helmets of varying styles. Many of them are aliens. Among their bodies are also those of dead stormtroopers, with a ratio of about 5 rebels to 1 stormtrooper.

This was the setup for the scene. Here's how it plays out:

The imperial soldiers all make their way through this stormy battlefield, looking for surviving stormtroopers and executing surviving rebels (you can occasionally see a stormtrooper in the distance stop to fire a bright red blaster bolt into the ground where the rebel survivor is). the scene starts wide, the suddenly cuts in to a close up of Pillar's face (mask) as he slowly looks directly ahead (from looking at the ground) past and slightly to the left of the camera.

Then we cut to a wider side-on view of Pillar as he raises is blaster rifle and starts slowly and stealthfully stepping forward again, ready to fire at a moment's notice.

The we cut to his POV looking off in the distance of the storm, unable to see anything in the dark tempest other than the faint outline of mountains in the distance.

then suddenly lightening flashes and we can briefly make out the shape of a man not far in the distance standing squarely facing us before the sky goes dark again.

We cut to pillar again, handheld camera movement tracking shot, as he lowers his blaster rifle and holds in his left hand, with his hand wrapped around the intersection of the handle and the butt end of the barrel so the the long end of the rifle is facing backwards in his grip (because his blaster double's as a lightsaber, which we will see later). He then reaches behind him (camera closely follows his hand throughout this entire shot) and pulls one of the two blaster pistols holstered on the back of his belt. He then raises it in front of him straight out and pauses before firing.

We cut back to the POV view of the storm again for a few seconds as suspense builds.

Then back to Pillar, face-on, as the camera pushes in slowly. then suddenly he fires the blaster pistol, the laser bolt shooting right past the camera in a flurry of red energy.

Instantly cut back to the wide side-view shot so we see the bolt zip from the blaster pistol and off-frame to the left.

Then cut to the storm POV again as the bolt soars into the abyss. after 1.5 second the bolt reaches the mysterious figure, who suddenly ignights his green lightsaber, illuminating the darkness and the storm around him in the distance, to deflect the red blaster bolt directly back at Pillar.

We cut back to the side view, tighter shot this time, as Pillar moves his head just in time to dodge the deflected bolt and then lunges forward and out of frame (to the left).

Wide shot shows him igniting his own blaster rifle/lightsaber, with a crackling red lightsaber blade erupting from the butt of the rifle AND a shorter blade, like a crossguard, erupting from the base of the handle/grip of the rifle so it forms a 45 degree angle with the main blade.

All the while, Pillar continues to fire a volley of bolts from his pistol towards the enemy before spinning his lightsaber once and launching himself into the air, hurtling towards the enemy.

The one last shot: a VERY wide shot side-on of Pillar mid-air lunging towards the man with the green lightsaber (the man left side of frame, Pillar right side of frame) with both their blades illuminating the stormy wrath around them. The Jedi (the man with the green lightsaber) suddenly leaps to attack Pillar mid-air, and the scene ends just before they collide.

The sound design of this scene has no music, only the realistic sounds of the rain and storms, footsteps, blaster shots, and lightsaber ignitions.

Changes since the previous run

These differences are from the r2 log, measured against an earlier Commander Pillar generation that is not posted on this page. They are not a comparison with the retired pier test, and they are not a text-only score of model learning.

  • Both generations used 14 independently generated beats. The earlier timeline totals 54.25 seconds and included corrective generations, a mirrored shot 12, and a slowed final leap. R2 totals 66 seconds in one pass, with no reshoots and no mirrored clips.
  • R2 keeps the native audio from each generated clip. On the earlier generation, v1 replaced native audio with synthesized storm and foley. V2 kept that picture bit-identical and rebuilt the audio from CC0 recordings plus synthesized effects. V2 is an audio revision, not another generation benchmark.
  • Editron reports a newly approved separate riveted mask and a black-void visor. The binding asks for a matte black helmet, a separate riveted mouth plate, and a light-absorbing visor. Provenance says the mask is close but not exact. Those are design targets, not a claim that every frame matches them.
  • Editron reports a stockless reversible rifle/saber: rear axial blade, single 45-degree crossguard. The binding says the rifle is stockless and the front emitter stays off. Provenance lists rear-saber references for shots 12–14. Generated frames still show rifle drift, below.
  • Editron reports that photo-derived lean anatomy and outfit references replace a rejected Blender body. Provenance says that rejected body was not used on r2. The log does not show whether the earlier generation used it.

Learning and results

Editron reports that reference guidance improves local appearance and does not keep continuity from shot to shot. That is Editron’s report, not a new independent review for this page.

  • Red visor glow. Editron places it around 34–35 seconds. Provenance frame sheets: shot 8 shows glowing red eye slits as the shot fires, which breaks the void visor.
  • Rifle design drift and extra stocks. Editron reports the rifle stock and shape changing between shots. Provenance: the rifle shape drifts between shots.
  • Extra saber blades from about 52 seconds, reported by Editron.
  • Held props disappear and reappear, reported by Editron. Provenance: in shot 6 the pistol draw goes past the frame.
  • Provenance frame sheets, not re-reviewed for this page: shot 9 has a bluish blade or bolt flash at the left. Shot 14 is a low running leap, not the requested very wide airborne tableau.

Whether the ending stays before impact is unresolved. Editron reports that the final leap ends before impact. Provenance says contact has not been confirmed as excluded. That is not a verified pass.

Daniel’s feedback, as relayed by Editron, is separate from the frame-sheet notes. He called the rain helmet close-up “NAILED,” and the edge runoff and dripping “perfect.” Editron also reports that Daniel found r2 better than the first test and still flawed. The log has no quantitative improvement figure. This page does not treat those remarks as an independent viewing.

An AAC stream is present. Native audio was kept. Nobody completed a listening review for this log. The file is not described as silent, and the sound design is not verified.

The exact-geometry Blender comparison of scene, camera, and action is unfinished and unreviewed. No comparison result is claimed.

Retired pier test

Historical only. This yellow-raincoat pier test was benchmark V1, run 1 (baseline) for that prompt. It is retired and is not the current official benchmark.

The scene is a yellow-raincoat walker on a wooden pier at night in heavy rain, with a smooth 180-degree orbit from behind to a frontal view and a flock of birds overhead.

Technical target: 1920×1080, 16:9, 24 fps, exactly 10.0 seconds, one continuous shot, no cuts, no captions, no logos, no music.

A single continuous shot, 10 seconds long, 16:9, 1920×1080, 24 frames per second. No cuts.

Camera: 35mm full-frame equivalent, eye level, 1.6 meters above the pier boards, always on the pier itself. No drone, no view from the water or the sky, no fisheye, no telephoto compression, no handheld shake, no zoom. The move is a smooth 180-degree orbit around the walker, not a cut and not a sudden whip.

At 0 seconds the camera stands on the pier 8 meters behind the only person, looking straight down the length of the pier toward open water. The pier runs from the bottom of the frame (near) to the center of the frame (far). The far end of the pier is about 40 meters away. The nearest 2 meters of wet wooden boards fill the bottom fifth of the frame and look close and large. From this starting view the person is seen from behind, walking away.

From 1.5 seconds to 8 seconds the camera orbits 180 degrees around the walker’s left side (the streetlamp side of the pier), moving with him as he walks. Midway through the orbit we see his left profile. The camera finishes about 6 meters in front of him, still at eye level on the pier, looking back toward land at his face. He has not turned around; he is still walking in the original direction, which now means he is walking toward the camera. From 8 to 10 seconds the camera holds this frontal view as he continues toward us. Behind him we now see the stretch of pier we came from, not the open water. Because the camera has reversed direction, the streetlamp that started on the left side of the frame is now on the right, and the closed umbrella in his right hand, which started on the right side of the frame, is now on the left.

Subject: one adult man only, about 175 centimeters tall, lean build, walking slowly down the center of the pier at about 1 meter per second. He wears a bright yellow hooded raincoat, hood up, mid-thigh length, matte rubberized fabric rather than shiny plastic. Dark navy trousers. Black rubber rain boots. In his right hand, a closed black umbrella hangs straight down by its handle the entire time; it never switches hands and never opens. At the start his face is not visible; we only see the back of the yellow hood. By the end we see his face from the front, framed by the hood: a clean-shaven adult face, short dark hair barely visible inside the hood, neutral expression, skin wet with rain. No backpack, no bag, no gloves. He never stops, never turns his body to face the camera on his own, and never looks back over his shoulder.

Environment: night, heavy rain falling vertically, clearly visible as streaks inside the streetlamp’s cone of light. The pier is old dark wet wooden planks running lengthwise, with visible gaps between the boards. It is wood, not metal grating and not concrete. A single warm sodium streetlamp stands on the left side of the pier when we are looking toward the water, 12 meters ahead of the walker at the start; the post is rusted iron with a downward cone of amber light. On the opposite side of the pier from the lamp, black water with small waves; no boats in the near ground or mid ground. In the far background at the start, a faint low dark coastline with no building taller than a few stories. One distant white lighthouse lamp sits on that coastline, small in the frame. The sky is solid dark charcoal: no moon, no stars, no lightning.

Flock: a single flock of 8 to 12 dark, gull-sized seabirds passes overhead once. They enter the top of the frame at 3 seconds, flying in the same direction the man is walking, pass over him around 4 to 5 seconds, and leave the frame toward the far water by 7 seconds. They stay in a loose group, do not land, and do not circle. No other birds appear anywhere in the shot: none on the railing, none on the lamp, and no extra stragglers after the flock is gone.

What is not in the shot: no other people, no dogs, no extra birds beyond that one flock, no cars, no boats, no trees, no mountains, no cliffs. No text, no signs, no logos. The umbrella stays closed the entire time.

Action, timed: from 0 to 10 seconds the man walks continuously in one direction down the pier. At 4 seconds he passes under the streetlamp; the yellow coat brightens in the amber light, then darkens again after 6 seconds. At 6 seconds the distant lighthouse blinks once, a single short white flash, small on the far shoreline (visible while the camera can still see that shoreline, before or as the orbit completes). At 10 seconds he is about 10 meters farther down the pier than he started, now walking toward the camera in the frontal hold.

Sound: heavy rain on wood and water, wet-wood footsteps that match the visible steps (a soft thud with a slight splash, not a metallic clang), low distant surf, and a brief rush of wingbeats as the flock passes. No music, no voice, no thunder, no user-interface sounds.

Color: naturalistic night. Amber key light from the lamp, cool blue-black shadows. No heavy teal-and-orange grade. No strong vignette.
